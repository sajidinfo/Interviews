# Podcast Conversation Script: Books
## Episode 03: Hosted by Sajid, Featuring Gulista

**Intro Music Plays (Energetic, inviting tune)**

**Sajid**:  
Welcome back to *Friends Talking Life*! I’m your host, Sajid, and I’m thrilled to be here with my fantastic friend, Gulista, for our third episode. Gulista, how’s it hanging?

**Gulista**:  
Hey, Sajid! I’m pumped to be back. I’ve got my imaginary bookshelf ready for this one—let’s talk books!

**Sajid**:  
Haha, love the enthusiasm! Today’s episode is all about books—those stories that pull us in, make us think, or just give us an escape. So, Gulista, what’s the first book that comes to mind when you think of your reading journey?

**Gulista**:  
Oh, that’s easy. *The Alchemist* by Paulo Coelho. I read it when I was 16, and it blew my mind. The whole idea of following your “Personal Legend” and listening to your heart? It hit me hard. I still go back to it when I need a reminder to chase my dreams. What’s yours?

**Sajid**:  
Nice pick! For me, it’s *The Hobbit* by J.R.R. Tolkien. I was maybe 12, and my uncle gave me his old, dog-eared copy. I got lost in Bilbo’s adventure—dragons, hobbits, that ring! It made me want to explore every corner of the world, real or not. Still does.

**Gulista**:  
That’s awesome! I can totally see you as a kid, dreaming of slaying dragons. So, what is it about *The Hobbit* that keeps it special for you?

**Sajid**:  
It’s the sense of adventure mixed with heart. Bilbo’s just this regular guy—well, hobbit—who steps out of his comfort zone and finds courage he didn’t know he had. Plus, Tolkien’s world feels so real, you know? Like I could walk into the Shire. What about *The Alchemist*? Why’s it your go-to?

**Gulista**:  
It’s the simplicity of the story, but also how deep it goes. It’s like a guide for life—trusting the journey, even when it’s scary or unclear. I remember reading it during a time when I wasn’t sure what I wanted to do with my life, and it gave me this quiet confidence to keep going. Plus, Coelho’s writing is like poetry.

**Sajid**:  
Totally get that. Okay, let’s talk about a book that didn’t quite hit the mark. Ever read something that everyone raved about, but you just couldn’t get into?

**Gulista**:  
Ugh, yes. *Eat, Pray, Love* by Elizabeth Gilbert. Everyone was obsessed, but I found it so… self-indulgent. Like, I get the whole self-discovery thing, but I couldn’t connect with her journey. I kept thinking, “Just eat the pasta and move on!” Your turn—what’s a book you just didn’t vibe with?

**Sajid**:  
Haha, that’s brutal! For me, it’s *Moby-Dick*. I know it’s a classic, but I got stuck on all the whale-hunting details. I was like, “Herman Melville, can we get back to the actual story?” I skimmed half of it. Don’t tell my English teacher.

**Gulista**:  
Your secret’s safe with me! Okay, let’s flip it—what’s a book that surprised you? Like, you didn’t expect to love it, but it hooked you.

**Sajid**:  
Good question. I’d say *The Night Circus* by Erin Morgenstern. I picked it up randomly because the cover looked cool, but the story? Magic, mystery, this dreamy vibe—it sucked me in. I didn’t want it to end. What’s your surprise hit?

**Gulista**:  
*Station Eleven* by Emily St. John Mandel. I’m not usually into post-apocalyptic stuff, but this one’s about art, survival, and human connection after a pandemic. I read it in one sitting and cried like a baby at the end. It’s haunting but hopeful.

**Sajid**:  
That sounds intense! Adding it to my list. So, let’s get personal—what’s a book that changed how you see the world?

**Gulista**:  
*Educated* by Tara Westover. Her story of growing up in a strict, isolated family and teaching herself enough to get to college—it’s mind-blowing. It made me think about how education can open doors and how much we take for granted. What about you?

**Sajid**:  
Man, that’s a powerful one. For me, it’s *1984* by George Orwell. It freaked me out, thinking about how easily truth can get twisted and how power can mess with your mind. It made me pay more attention to what’s going on around me, you know, question things.

**Gulista**:  
Totally. That book’s a wake-up call. Alright, fun question—what book character do you wish you could hang out with in real life?

**Sajid**:  
Easy—Gandalf from *The Lord of the Rings*. Dude’s wise, has epic stories, and probably throws the best parties in Middle-earth. Imagine chilling with him, sipping tea, talking about life. Who’s your pick?

**Gulista**:  
I’m going with Hermione Granger from *Harry Potter*. She’s smart, fierce, and would totally help me organize my life. Plus, she’d probably teach me a spell or two. We’d be besties.

**Sajid**:  
Haha, you and Hermione would be unstoppable! Okay, as we wrap up, what’s one book you’d recommend to everyone listening, and why?

**Gulista**:  
I’d say *The Alchemist*. It’s short, it’s profound, and it’s for anyone who’s ever felt a little lost or needed a nudge to follow their heart. What’s your pick, Sajid?

**Sajid**:  
I’m going with *The Hobbit*. It’s a classic adventure that reminds you it’s never too late to step out and try something new. Plus, it’s just a fun ride. Alright, Gulista, this was a blast!

**Gulista**:  
So much fun! Thanks for having me, Sajid. Let’s do this again.

**Sajid**:  
Absolutely! To our listeners, we want to hear about your favorite books or ones that changed your life—drop them in the comments or send us a message. That’s it for this episode of *Friends Talking Life*. See you next time!

**Outro Music Plays (Warm, reflective tune)**
